## Phase 2: Answer the model-and-units questions

**Decision recommended: use O1 as a working approximation.** Define the states directly in thousands of pelts and add the observation equations

\[
Y_{H,i}=H(t_i)+\varepsilon_{H,i},\qquad
Y_{P,i}=P(t_i)+\varepsilon_{P,i},\qquad t_i=\mathrm{Year}_i-1900.
\]

Under O1, H = cH NH and P = cP NP, where N is latent animal abundance and cH,cP are unknown positive constants. We fit the effective pelt-state equations, without fitting those constants. This does not assume one pelt represents one animal in the model's numerical units. Annual totals are treated as point observations, as explicitly proposed by Advay. Source: [observation convention, sections 1–2](https://github.com/FriedelCraft/lynx-hare-project/blob/bcc0a2b1ba4a277712ccd43ee3ff81e1397e7f03/docs/advay_observation_convention.md).

**Reason and tradeoff.** The constant scale transformation preserves the Rosenzweig–MacArthur equation form, as derived in our [units sheet](equations_and_units.md). It gives the two fitting methods a common observable state definition. A latent-population model with estimated trapping factors, or an integral over each trapping season, would introduce additional assumptions and quantities. The simpler convention sacrifices absolute-population interpretation and an explicit account of the annual observation interval. Constancy of the observation factors is assumed, not established by the fit.

**Interpretation.** The transformation leaves r, m, β = b/K, α = ea/r and δ = m/r unchanged. Joel's σ = K/K_H is also unchanged where the coexistence threshold is applicable. K, b, a and e are effective pelt-unit coefficients. r and m can be interpreted as the model's growth and mortality rates conditional on O1 and on an adequate dynamical model. Scale invariance alone does not prove accurate biological recovery. The dimensionless groups describe model dynamics with the same qualification. This is a consequence of our [unit-change derivation](equations_and_units.md), using [Joel's threshold definition](https://github.com/FriedelCraft/lynx-hare-project/blob/bcc0a2b1ba4a277712ccd43ee3ff81e1397e7f03/report/joel_stability_section.tex).

**a versus e.** With the observed predator scale fixed, the free rescaling of P is no longer an admissible way to leave the same observations unchanged. Also, a occurs in the prey equation and ea in the predator equation. Thus the scale argument alone does not show that effective a and e are inseparable. It does not prove that all six parameters can be reliably estimated from 16 annual pairs. Fixing e is an inference decision for Joel, Advay and Yash, supported by training-data diagnostics and explicit assumptions. No e ≤ 1 restriction follows from its effective units. Sources: [model equations](equations_and_units.md), [Advay's question](https://github.com/FriedelCraft/lynx-hare-project/blob/bcc0a2b1ba4a277712ccd43ee3ff81e1397e7f03/docs/advay_observation_convention.md).

**Result.** The units sheet now includes the observation equation, states what ε means, and distinguishes thousands of pelts from thousands of pelts per year in a derivative. It does not assert that normalized residual weights are empirically estimated noise variances.
